socks vs AI images

2026-05-26

TL;DR

  • Electricity: 1 pair of socks ≈ 100–1,000 AI images (100× electricity-only, 1,000× if you count thermal energy)
  • Water (blue, like-for-like): 1 pair of socks ≈ ~1,000 AI images
  • Water (full cradle-to-gate, including pasture rainfall): 1 pair of socks ≈ hundreds of thousands of AI images

A pair of merino wool socks and an AI-generated image are separated by roughly two to three orders of magnitude in resource use, though the exact ratio depends heavily on what you count. On electricity, the most-cited peer-reviewed study of generative image models (Luccioni et al., 2023) measured an average of ~3 Wh per image across tested diffusion models, while a 70g pair of merino socks requires somewhere around 300–500 Wh of electricity for spinning, knitting, and finishing — a ratio of roughly 100×. If you include thermal energy from scouring and dyeing (the natural gas and steam that dominate textile processing), the sock's total cradle-to-gate energy climbs to 2–6 kWh, pushing the ratio closer to 1,000×. On water, the gap is wider: research from UC Riverside puts a generated image at ~15–60 mL of total freshwater (on-site cooling plus the indirect water used to generate the electricity), while a pair of socks consumes roughly 50 liters of directly-withdrawn freshwater for processing and 15,000–25,000 liters if you include all the rainfall on the pasture the sheep grazed (the standard “water footprint” methodology). That's a ratio of ~1,000× on a blue-water basis and several hundred thousand on a full cradle-to-gate basis. The headline ratios sound dramatic, but the comparison cuts both ways: most of the sock's water is rain that would have fallen anyway, while a meaningful share of data center water is withdrawn from already-stressed aquifers, so the per-liter impact isn't equivalent even when volumes are.

Sources

  • Luccioni, Jernite & Strubell (2023), “Power Hungry Processing: Watts Driving the Cost of AI Deployment?” — arXiv:2311.16863
  • Li, Yang, Islam & Ren (2023), “Making AI Less Thirsty: Uncovering and Addressing the Secret Water Footprint of AI Models” — arXiv:2304.03271
  • Mekonnen & Hoekstra (2012), “A Global Assessment of the Water Footprint of Farm Animal Products,” Ecosystems 15:401–415 — Water Footprint Network
  • Wiedemann et al. (2020), “Environmental impacts associated with the production, use, and end-of-life of a woollen garment,” International Journal of LCA
  • IEEE Spectrum (Sept 2025), “The Real Story on AI Water Usage at Data Centers”
  • MIT Technology Review (May 2025), “We did the math on AI's energy footprint”

Caveat: the AI numbers are averages across model families and will keep dropping as hardware and models improve; the sock numbers are also averages across very different supply chains (Australian extensive grazing looks nothing like Chinese intensive production). Treat as order-of-magnitude.
